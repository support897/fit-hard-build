# Fit App — Audience Research for Board v2 + Companion Invention

Written by the Fit App Build desk, 14 Oct 2026, on Ilse's
ruling (board v2 waits on real audience research; the Lab's
companion invention pass grounds in it). Sources are named
per finding; anything not evidenced is marked
"could not verify". Quotes are brief; findings are
paraphrased.

## The audiences, sized

- **The intimidated are the market, not a niche.**
  GymNation's 2026 Fitness Report: 47% name fear of
  judgement the number-one reason they do not join or
  return to a gym. PureGym research: about 1 in 5 won't go
  at all because they feel intimidated. OnePoll for The
  Gym Group (3,000 UK non-gym adults): 33% suffer
  "gymtimidation"; 29% have been made uncomfortable by not
  knowing how to use equipment; a near-quarter say not
  knowing where to start puts them off. An ASICS/Mind
  survey of non-exercisers: 68% too embarrassed to go,
  78% do not find typical sports advertising motivating,
  42% say boastful exercise posts make them feel a failure
  before they start.
- **GLP-1 users** are a fast-consolidating companion-app
  market (e.g. Glowise's launch, Oct 2026): the winning
  shape is ONE home for dose logs + meals + symptoms +
  how-they-felt, with the lowest possible logging effort
  (voice, photo). Vendor framing, but it matches the
  product pattern across the category. Direct community
  voice on preferred app TONE for this group: could not
  verify beyond the general shame evidence below.

## What the evidence says they respond to

1. **Calm beats energy for this audience.** The mainstream
   shelf sells energy (Strava orange, Nike black, neon) —
   and the intimidated majority explicitly does not find
   that register motivating (78%, above). Environmental
   psychology summaries used in clinical design: light
   blue lowers anxiety; green reads as balance and safety;
   soft beige/cream is neutral, grounding, non-threatening;
   bright red raises arousal and bright yellow
   overstimulates; stark white feels clinical and cold.
   Muted, nature-adjacent palettes (sage, warm taupe, soft
   blue-grey) are the recurring wellness register in
   practice, with warm cream grounds — NOT multi-hue brights.
2. **Fewer colours, one system.** The loved gentle apps
   are visually restrained: Finch is "intentionally
   stripped down... no overwhelming menus" (review
   consensus), Gentler Streak replaces rings and loud
   gamification with soft graphs against your own norm.
   Ilse's "too messy" read on v1 sits exactly on this
   evidence: four saturated area colours + amber reads as
   several apps at once to an audience that wants one
   quiet room.
3. **Shame is the churn mechanism.** UCL/Loughborough
   (British Journal of Health Psychology, 2025): 58,881
   posts about five top fitness apps; users reported shame
   logging "unhealthy" foods, irritation at nagging
   notifications, disappointment at rigid algorithmic
   targets — leading to demotivation and giving up. The
   researchers' prescription: kinder, holistic design,
   intrinsic motivation over rigid numbers. Any visual
   language that feels like a scoreboard (alarm reds,
   broken-streak iconography, leaderboard energy) works
   against retention in this audience.
4. **Permission to rest is a feature users name.**
   Gentler Streak (Apple Watch App of the Year 2022; 2024
   Apple Design Award, Social Impact) built its whole
   identity on adapting to the day and letting rest keep
   the streak; long-term users credit "permission and
   encouragement to drop the guilt" for the longest
   adherence they have ever had to a fitness app. That is
   this app's morning check-in mechanic, already built —
   the visual language should say the same thing.
5. **Guide figures work — when they never judge and the
   user makes them theirs.** Finch (10M+ downloads;
   ~76% female, mostly 25–35; 2.34M downloads in one
   recent 90-day window): users adopt and NAME their bird,
   customise its colour, and describe the bond as the
   reason they return; the app "doesn't judge you, it just
   supports you", and its design is deliberately simple.
   The counter-pole is documented too: mascot-plus-guilt
   mechanics (Duolingo's streak pressure) produce stress,
   self-recrimination and resentment in the research
   literature on gamification misuse (arXiv 2203.16175).
   The figure is not the risk; the DISAPPOINTMENT is.
   Design rules that follow: the companion must never be
   sad at the user, never shrink, starve, or sicken on a
   missed day, and its happiest state must be reachable on
   a rest day.
6. **Round, soft geometry is the kindness register.**
   Store Review's shelf research (LOGO-RESEARCH, pattern
   6): round beats sharp in care/kindness categories,
   sharp beats round in performance ones. This audience
   is buying care, not performance.

## What this means for board v2 (the desk's synthesis)

- **Palette:** one warm ground (cream family), one ink,
  ONE lead colour drawn from the calm register (soft
  blue-green / sage / teal family — the clinical-calm +
  trust evidence points there), and at most one warm
  accent used sparingly. Area identity, where it survives
  at all, should come from iconography and tints of the
  single lead, not four saturated hues. Fewer, tidier —
  her words and the evidence agree.
- **Energy:** quiet confidence. No neon, no alarm colours,
  no scoreboard red anywhere near food or weight.
- **Type:** adult and friendly; the child-adjacent risk
  the Lab flagged in Baloo is a real axis for this
  audience (adults managing medication and weight do not
  want a kids' app) — v2 type should read warm but grown.
- **Companion territory for the Lab's invention:** a
  figure the user NAMES and can lightly customise; soft,
  round, unhurried; present-tense companion, not a coach
  and not a pet that suffers; its states mirror the user's
  day (rest is a good state, visibly). What users already
  love says the attachment comes from care loops and
  zero judgement, not from cuteness alone.
